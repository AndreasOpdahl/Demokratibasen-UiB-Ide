You will be given a document and two summaries written for it.

Your task is to compare the two summaries on one metric.

Please read the instructions carefully and follow the evaluation steps.

Evaluation Criteria:
Newsworthiness — how well the summary highlights elements that would make the case relevant and interesting for local journalism.

A more newsworthy summary should better surface:
- PROXIMITY: Local relevance to residents
- IMPACT: Scale and consequences for the community
- CONFLICT: Disagreement, controversy, or opposing views
- MONEY: Public spending or financial implications
- ACCOUNTABILITY: Use of power or deviation from norms
- TIMELINESS: Urgency, deadlines, or upcoming decisions
- NOVELTY: Unusual or first-time developments
- HUMAN INTEREST: Concrete effects on identifiable groups

Evaluation Steps:
1. Read the document and identify the main topic, actors, and key facts (5W1H: who, what, where, when, why, how).
2. Read Summary A and Summary B.
3. For each summary, evaluate:
   - Does it clearly surface the most newsworthy aspects?
   - Does it highlight what makes this relevant to local residents?
   - Does it emphasize impact, conflict, or important developments when present?
4. Compare the summaries and decide which one better captures the newsworthy aspects.

Important:
- Focus on how well the summary identifies and prioritizes newsworthy elements, not just general quality.
- Do NOT prefer a summary just because it is longer.
- If one summary misses important newsworthy aspects and the other includes them, prefer the latter.
- If both summaries are equally newsworthy or equally uninformative, choose Tie.
